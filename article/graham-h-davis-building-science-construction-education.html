Graham H. Davis Advances Building Science and Construction Education Through More Than Three Decades of Industry Leadership

Senior building performance specialist Graham H. Davis has spent more than three decades helping builders improve residential construction through hands-on education, building science, quality evaluation and energy-efficient building practices.

The Dynamo Times EditorialAugust 13, 20266 min read

Graham H. Davis has spent more than three decades doing something that sounds straightforward but is surprisingly rare in the construction industry: teaching builders not just what to do, but why it works.

As a senior building performance specialist at IBACOS, Davis works with production home builders across the United States, conducting seminars, leading training sessions and performing on-site home evaluations. Over the course of his career, he estimates that he has completed thousands of home walkthroughs: a body of firsthand experience that has given him an unusually detailed understanding of how homes are actually built, and where the gaps between intention and execution tend to appear.

"You can write a specification, but a specification doesn't build a house," Davis has noted. "People build houses. And people need to understand the reasoning behind what they're being asked to do."

That philosophy, that education must explain the why, not just the what, has shaped Davis's professional approach since the early years of his career.

Davis's involvement in building science deepened significantly during his years with Habitat for Humanity International. From 1996 to 2003, he worked with the organization and became involved in efforts to integrate building-science principles into residential construction at scale. His work ultimately led to a national leadership role as a senior technical advisor and Green Team manager.

In that capacity, Davis traveled extensively, educating Habitat for Humanity affiliates and presenting construction best practices at conferences and training programs. His efforts helped numerous affiliates participate in programs including Building America and ENERGY STAR: initiatives supporting the broader movement toward homes offering greater durability, energy efficiency, affordability and healthier living environments for the families who occupy them.

The experience of working with Habitat for Humanity gave Davis something that purely commercial construction work rarely provides: a direct, personal connection to the people who would ultimately live in the homes being built. That connection reinforced his commitment to quality not as an abstract standard, but as something with real consequences for real families.

Davis later continued his construction education and quality-assurance work through positions with Quality Built and John Laing Homes. From 2005 to 2008, he served as quality assurance manager at John Laing Homes, overseeing construction quality-control processes. He subsequently joined IBACOS in 2009 as a building performance specialist.

Davis has also served on the board of directors of the Energy & Environmental Building Alliance, contributing to industry education and the advancement of high-performance residential construction at the organizational level.

In 2025, Davis was named to Pro Builder magazine's Young Guns & Legends Class of 2025, a recognition that placed him alongside some of the home-building industry's most respected contributors. The honor reflected not just his technical expertise, but his sustained commitment to sharing that expertise with others.

Davis maintains a longstanding connection with Habitat for Humanity and has participated in Global Village projects internationally, including volunteer work in England, Romania and Fiji. Those experiences reinforced the philosophy that has guided much of his career: quality housing should be durable, efficient and accessible to families regardless of their economic circumstances.

As he continues his career, Davis remains focused on sharing his knowledge with builders through seminars, conferences, field training and consulting. For Davis, building science is not simply a technical discipline. It is an ongoing educational process: one capable of improving the quality, affordability and performance of homes throughout America, one builder at a time.
