Surgeon Dr. Priya Sharma Pioneers Procedure That Saves Thousands

Her minimally invasive cardiac technique is now standard practice at over 200 hospitals nationwide.

Marcus WebbAugust 11, 20265 min read

In the operating room, Dr. Priya Sharma moves with a precision that her colleagues describe as almost architectural. Every incision is deliberate. Every decision is made with the full weight of what is at stake.

That precision has produced results that have changed the standard of care for cardiac patients across the country. The minimally invasive technique she developed, now known as the Sharma Protocol, has been adopted by more than 200 hospitals nationwide and is credited with reducing post-operative complications by 34%.

"I didn't set out to change the standard of care," Sharma says. "I set out to help my patients recover faster and with less pain. The rest followed from that."

The Sharma Protocol replaces a traditional open-chest approach with a series of small incisions and specialized instruments that allow surgeons to perform complex cardiac repairs without the trauma of a full sternotomy. Patients typically leave the hospital two to three days sooner than with conventional surgery and return to normal activity in half the time.

The technique required years of development and refinement. Sharma spent seven years working with engineers, instrument designers, and fellow surgeons to develop the tools and training protocols required to make the procedure reproducible across different surgical teams.

"The hardest part wasn't the surgery itself," she says. "It was making it teachable. A technique that only I can perform doesn't help anyone. A technique that 500 surgeons can perform: that changes medicine."
