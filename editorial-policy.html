Editorial Policy

What we publish, how we check it, how this publication is paid for, and what payment does not buy.

Last updated October 16, 2026

01What We Publish

The Dynamo Times covers the professional record of people who lead, build and practise: executives, physicians, attorneys, academics, engineers, founders and public servants. We publish three kinds of material.

  • Press releases. Announcements of a professional milestone, appointment, award or body of work, written in our own words from material supplied to us and from public sources.
  • Professional profiles. A structured record of a person career, credentials and work.
  • Features and interviews. Longer editorial pieces.

We cover professional life. We do not report on the private lives of the people we feature, and we do not run material whose purpose is to attack a named individual or a competitor.

02How We Are Funded, Said Plainly

A reader deserves to know how a publication pays for itself before deciding how much weight to give it, so here it is without euphemism.

A large part of what we publish is paid placement. Individuals and organisations pay for a press release or a professional profile to be written and published. That is the commercial basis of this publication.

We think that model is legitimate, and we also think it is only legitimate if we are straightforward about it. So:

  • Payment buys consideration, writing and publication. It does not buy a claim we cannot support.
  • Payment does not buy a favourable adjective, an invented credential or a number nobody can verify.
  • Payment does not buy removal of a correction once a correction is warranted.
  • We do not take payment to publish material about somebody who has not agreed to be featured.

Read our Sponsored Content Policy for how paid material is labelled.

03Verification

Before we publish a claim about a person, we expect it to be supportable. In practice that means:

  • Credentials and titles are taken from the person, their employer, a professional registry or a public record, and written as those sources state them.
  • Dates and durations come from the same places. Where a span is approximate we say more than, not an exact figure we cannot stand behind.
  • Awards and recognitions are named specifically, with the awarding body, so a reader can check them.
  • Superlatives such as leading, foremost or top are avoided unless a named source said it, in which case we attribute it to that source rather than asserting it ourselves.

Where we cannot support a claim, we leave it out. That is sometimes an uncomfortable conversation with the person featured, and we have it anyway.

04Use of AI Tools

We use software, including AI assisted tools, in parts of our production: drafting assistance, editing, formatting, image preparation and site maintenance.

We do not publish material that no person has read. Every article that carries our name has been reviewed by a human being before publication, and the facts in it are our responsibility regardless of what tool helped produce the draft.

We do not use AI tools to invent people, quotations, organisations, credentials or events. A quotation on this site was said by the person it is attributed to.

05Photographs and Images

Photographs of the people we feature are supplied by them or used with permission. Where a person has no usable photograph, we use a plain graphic placeholder rather than a stock image of a stranger.

We crop, resize and adjust exposure so that portraits sit consistently on the page. We do not alter the content of a photograph: nothing is added, removed or rearranged, and we do not generate a synthetic image of a real person and present it as a photograph.

06Editorial Independence

Editorial decisions are made by the editorial desk. A commercial relationship does not give anyone a veto over whether a correction is published, how a fact is stated, or what we decline to print.

We reserve the right to decline any submission, to stop work on a commissioned piece, and to decline to continue covering a person or organisation. We do not have to explain the reason, although we usually will.

07Complaints and Right of Reply

If we have published something about you that is wrong, unfair, or missing context that changes its meaning, write to admin@topdynamos.com. Set out the page, the passage and what is wrong with it.

A person criticised in an article is entitled to respond, and we will carry a fair response.

A person is read by a human being, not a template. If we got it wrong we will say so on the page. If we disagree, we will tell you that and why, rather than ignoring you.
