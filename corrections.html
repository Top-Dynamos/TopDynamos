Corrections Policy

How to tell us we got something wrong, what we do about it, and how a change is marked so readers can see it happened.

Last updated October 16, 2026

01The Principle

We get things wrong. Every publication does. What separates a publication worth reading from one that is not is what happens next.

When we are told we have published something inaccurate, we check it. If it is wrong, we fix it, and we say on the page that we fixed it. We do not quietly edit an error out of existence and hope nobody noticed, because a reader who saw the original deserves to know it changed.

02How to Report an Error

Email admin@topdynamos.com with the subject line Correction. Telephone +1 (631) 824-0352 if you would rather talk to somebody.

It helps us a great deal if you include:

  • The link to the page.
  • The sentence or passage that is wrong, quoted.
  • What the correct information is.
  • Anything that supports it, if you have it to hand. A registry entry, an employer page, a dated document.

You do not have to be the person the article is about to report an error. Anyone can.

03What Happens Next

We aim to acknowledge every report within two working days, and to resolve it within five. Where a claim is harder to check we will tell you that rather than let the thread go quiet.

  • If you are right, we correct the page and add a dated correction note to it.
  • If the point is about emphasis or opinion rather than fact, we will say so, and we will offer to carry your response.
  • If we cannot establish either way, we will tell you what we found and what we are doing. Where a disputed claim cannot be supported, we remove the claim.

04How We Mark a Change

Correction

Used when a fact was wrong. The page carries a dated note saying what was wrong and what it now says. The note stays on the page permanently.

Clarification

Used when nothing was factually wrong but the wording could reasonably be read to mean something we did not intend. The page carries a dated note explaining the point.

Update

Used when the facts have moved on since publication: a role has changed, an organisation has been renamed. The article was right when published, so this is a note, not a correction.

Silent changes

We fix typographical errors, broken links and formatting faults without a note, because nothing a reader relied on has changed. Anything that alters meaning gets a note.

05Requests to Remove a Page

From time to time someone asks us to take a page down entirely rather than correct it.

We consider every request and we grant some. We do not treat removal as automatic, because a publication that deletes accurate work on request is not keeping a record, it is keeping a noticeboard.

We weigh how accurate the material is, how old it is, whether the person is in public professional life, whether real harm is being caused, and whether a correction or an added update would address the problem instead. Where we decline, we say why. Where privacy law gives you a right that applies, we honour it, and the Privacy Policy explains that route.

06Errors We Find Ourselves

The same rules apply when nobody has complained and we notice the mistake on our own. A correction note is added in the same way. An error does not become less of an error because the only person who spotted it works here.
