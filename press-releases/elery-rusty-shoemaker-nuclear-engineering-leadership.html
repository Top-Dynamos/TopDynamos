Elery M. "Rusty" Shoemaker, EdD, PMP, Builds a 45-Year Legacy of Leadership in Nuclear Power and Engineering

Engineering and nuclear-power professional Elery M. "Rusty" Shoemaker has devoted more than four decades to nuclear operations, safety, leadership development, training and organizational excellence, including extensive service with INPO and WANO.

INPO / WANOAcworth, GeorgiaAugust 31, 2026

ACWORTH, Ga.: Dynamo Times highlights Elery M. "Rusty" Shoemaker, EdD, PMP, an engineering and nuclear-power professional whose career spans approximately 45 years of work in commercial nuclear operations, training, peer review, leadership development and organizational improvement.

Since 1995, Dr. Shoemaker has held positions associated with the Institute of Nuclear Power Operations (INPO) and the World Association of Nuclear Operators (WANO), with his professional profile identifying him as a senior corporate team leader. His career reflects an uncommon combination of technical engineering education and advanced study in organizational leadership. That combination has allowed him to contribute not only to the technical side of nuclear power, but also to the people, teams and leadership systems responsible for operating nuclear facilities safely and reliably.

Few industries demand the level of discipline associated with commercial nuclear power. Nuclear facilities depend on technical accuracy, procedural compliance, rigorous training, operational excellence and a strong safety culture. Dr. Shoemaker has spent approximately 45 years working within that environment. His career began in commercial nuclear power in 1980 and developed through plant operations, training, accreditation, peer review and corporate leadership. Across those decades, the technology and regulatory environment evolved, but the industry's central responsibilities remained constant: safe operation, reliability and continuous learning.

Dr. Shoemaker's academic foundation combines physics and mechanical engineering. He earned a Bachelor of Arts in Physics from Thomas More University in 1979. He then earned a Bachelor of Science in Mechanical Engineering from the University of Kentucky in 1980. Physics provided a foundation for understanding the principles underlying energy and nuclear systems. Mechanical engineering added practical knowledge involving machinery, thermal systems, structures and engineering design. Together, the disciplines provided a strong technical platform for a career in nuclear power.

From 1980 to 1995, Dr. Shoemaker held a series of positions with Carolina Power & Light, the utility that later became associated with Progress Energy and Duke Energy. These 15 years established the operational foundation for the career that followed. Commercial nuclear operations require professionals to work within highly structured systems where technical performance, training and decision-making can have significant consequences. The experience provided Dr. Shoemaker with firsthand knowledge of nuclear-plant operations before he moved into broader industry oversight and leadership roles.

Dr. Shoemaker's professional record also identifies a Senior Reactor Operator credential for the H. B. Robinson nuclear facility, issued in 1983. Senior reactor operator qualification represents substantial knowledge of plant systems, operating procedures and nuclear operations. This practical plant background later became valuable as his responsibilities expanded into training, accreditation, peer review and industry leadership. Leadership in nuclear power carries particular credibility when it is grounded in direct operational experience.

In 1995, Dr. Shoemaker began the next major phase of his career with INPO and WANO. INPO was created to promote excellence in the operation of commercial nuclear power plants in the United States. WANO connects nuclear operators internationally with the goal of improving nuclear safety and reliability through cooperation, peer review and shared operating experience. Dr. Shoemaker's work across these organizations expanded his influence from individual plant operations to broader industry performance.

His professional profile identifies responsibility for leading Corporate Peer Review teams within INPO and the WANO Atlanta Centre. Peer reviews provide organizations with an opportunity to evaluate performance through experienced outside perspectives. The objective is not simply to identify shortcomings: effective peer review can help organizations understand strengths, recognize gaps, compare practices and establish priorities for improvement. Leading such teams requires technical credibility, communication skills and the ability to work with senior leaders across complex organizations.

The 2025 Marquis profile credits Dr. Shoemaker's work with INPO and WANO with fostering leadership in the safe and reliable operation of nuclear power internationally. Safety and reliability are closely connected in the nuclear industry. Reliable organizations develop disciplined processes, train people thoroughly, investigate events, identify weaknesses and learn from experience. Dr. Shoemaker's career has involved many of these organizational systems, particularly leadership, training and performance evaluation.

Engineering organizations can invest heavily in equipment and technology, but technical systems still depend on people. Dr. Shoemaker's career increasingly focused on this human side of nuclear excellence. His professional profile emphasizes a passion for teaching and leadership development. That focus recognizes an important principle: even highly engineered environments require people who can communicate expectations, make sound decisions, learn from mistakes and build effective teams. Technical excellence and leadership excellence are not competing priorities: in nuclear power, they reinforce one another.

After decades of technical and operational experience, Dr. Shoemaker returned to formal education to deepen his understanding of leadership. He earned a Master of Science in Organizational Leadership from Nova Southeastern University in 2012. He later completed an EdD in Organizational Leadership at Nova Southeastern University in 2022. Thomas More University independently noted the completion of his doctorate in its alumni publication. The advanced degrees added academic leadership theory to decades of practical nuclear-industry experience.

Organizational leadership examines how individuals, teams and institutions function. Topics can include communication, culture, decision-making, motivation, organizational change and leadership development. For a professional already working in nuclear operations, these concepts have immediate practical relevance. A nuclear organization may have excellent procedures, but leadership determines how consistently those procedures are reinforced. A company may collect operating experience, but culture influences whether employees feel responsible for learning from it. Dr. Shoemaker's career connects these organizational questions with a deeply technical industry.

Dr. Shoemaker earned the Project Management Professional (PMP) certification from the Project Management Institute in 2005. Project management provides structured approaches for planning, execution, coordination, risk management and completion. These capabilities are valuable in any large technical organization. Within nuclear power, projects frequently involve multiple disciplines, detailed schedules, regulatory requirements and significant operational considerations. The PMP credential added another formal dimension to Dr. Shoemaker's professional development.

Dr. Shoemaker's professional record also includes certifications from the National Academy for Nuclear Training. These include INPO event-investigation certification in 2002 and INPO instructor and facilitator certifications in 2003. Training is central to nuclear-industry performance: operators and technical professionals must maintain knowledge, practice responses and demonstrate proficiency. Facilitation is equally important because organizational learning often depends on productive discussions about performance, events and opportunities for improvement.

Dr. Shoemaker has also contributed to professional discussions surrounding nuclear training and accreditation. The official program for the 2017 American Nuclear Society Conference on Nuclear Training and Education lists Elery M. "Rusty" Shoemaker of INPO as presenting an update on recent changes to INPO accreditation. The appearance reflects his involvement in the systems used to evaluate and strengthen nuclear training programs. Training accreditation helps ensure that educational programs are structured to support the competencies needed in nuclear operations.

Technical expertise alone does not guarantee that a leader can influence an organization. Leaders must also communicate. Dr. Shoemaker's development in this area is reflected in his recognition as a Distinguished Toastmaster by Toastmasters International in 2013. The Distinguished Toastmaster designation represents advanced achievement within Toastmasters' communication and leadership programs. For someone responsible for leading teams, facilitating reviews and developing industry professionals, communication is not a secondary skill: it is part of the work.

Dr. Shoemaker has maintained involvement with professional and academic organizations throughout his career. His profile identifies affiliations with the Project Management Institute, American Nuclear Society, Golden Key International Honour Society and Delta Epsilon Iota Academic Honor Society. He is identified as a lifetime member of the American Nuclear Society. Professional organizations allow specialists to remain connected with developments beyond their immediate workplace and provide opportunities for technical exchange, education and collaboration.

Dr. Shoemaker's professional record includes multiple forms of recognition from INPO. His publicly available professional profile lists an INPO World Class Award, received in 2004. It also identifies multiple Above & Beyond Awards and Team Excellence Awards. These recognitions reflect performance within an organization whose mission centers on nuclear operational excellence and illustrate the sustained nature of Dr. Shoemaker's contributions across many years.

In 2022, Dr. Shoemaker was named a Kentucky Colonel by the Governor of Kentucky. The Kentucky Colonel designation is an honorary appointment recognizing noteworthy accomplishments and service. For Dr. Shoemaker, the recognition added a civic distinction to a career primarily associated with engineering, nuclear power and leadership development.

Dr. Shoemaker's commitment to leadership and teaching extends into community life. His professional profile identifies service as a deacon and Sunday school teacher at Burnt Hickory Baptist Church. It also notes contributions to community-welfare organizations including the Atlanta Community Food Bank and The Salvation Army. These activities reflect a service-oriented dimension of his leadership philosophy outside the nuclear industry.

Nuclear power is often discussed as a high-reliability industry because failures can carry significant consequences. High-reliability organizations place extraordinary emphasis on learning, questioning assumptions, operational discipline and continuous improvement. Dr. Shoemaker's professional path touches each of these themes. Plant operations gave him technical experience. Training taught him how knowledge is transferred. Peer review exposed him to organizational performance across facilities. Advanced leadership education provided frameworks for understanding culture and teams. Together, these experiences created a multidisciplinary approach to nuclear leadership.

Work involving WANO adds an international dimension to Dr. Shoemaker's career. Nuclear facilities around the world operate under different regulatory systems and organizational structures, but they share common responsibilities involving safety and reliability. International peer review and operating-experience exchange allow organizations to learn from events and practices outside their own national boundaries. Dr. Shoemaker's work has contributed to that broader culture of shared learning.

According to his 2025 professional profile, Dr. Shoemaker intends to continue supporting the nuclear-power industry after retirement through participation on Nuclear Safety Review Boards. Such boards can benefit from professionals who bring decades of operational and leadership experience. For Dr. Shoemaker, the plan represents continuity rather than a complete departure from the field: the setting may change, but the objective remains connected to nuclear safety, leadership and organizational performance.

Dynamo Times recognizes Elery M. "Rusty" Shoemaker, EdD, PMP, for his contributions to engineering, nuclear-power operations, training, organizational leadership and the continued advancement of nuclear safety and reliability.

About INPO / WANO

Elery M. "Rusty" Shoemaker, EdD, PMP, is an engineering and nuclear-power professional with approximately 45 years of industry experience. His career includes 15 years with Carolina Power & Light and, since 1995, a series of roles associated with the Institute of Nuclear Power Operations and World Association of Nuclear Operators. He earned a BA in Physics from Thomas More University, a BS in Mechanical Engineering from the University of Kentucky, an MS in Organizational Leadership from Nova Southeastern University and an EdD in Organizational Leadership from Nova Southeastern University. His professional credentials include Project Management Professional certification and nuclear-industry training, facilitation and event-investigation credentials.
