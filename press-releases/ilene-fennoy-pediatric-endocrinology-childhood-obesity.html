Ilene Fennoy, MD, MPH, Advances Pediatric Endocrinology Through Clinical Care, Obesity Research and Medical Leadership

Columbia University Professor of Pediatrics Ilene Fennoy combines specialty endocrine care, childhood-obesity research, adolescent bariatric medicine and the POWER Program for Overweight Education and Reduction.

Columbia University Irving Medical CenterNew York, NYSeptember 22, 2026

Dynamo Times highlights Ilene Fennoy, MD, MPH, a board-certified pediatric endocrinologist and Professor of Pediatrics at Columbia University Irving Medical Center whose career combines specialty medical care, nutrition, childhood-obesity research, education and program leadership.

Columbia identifies Dr. Fennoy as Medical Director of the Comprehensive Adolescent Bariatric Surgery Program and Director of the POWER Program for Overweight Education and Reduction. Her research focuses particularly on childhood obesity, its comorbidities, insulin resistance, bone health and the effects of interventions in adolescents with severe obesity. A 2026 Marquis Who's Who profile recognized Dr. Fennoy for expertise in pediatric endocrinology and her contributions to the health of children and adolescents.

Pediatric endocrinology addresses disorders involving hormones, growth, puberty, metabolism, bone health, thyroid function and diabetes. For children and adolescents, these conditions occur during periods of rapid development, making specialty care especially complex. Dr. Fennoy's career has focused on helping young patients and families navigate endocrine conditions while studying how metabolic disease can influence health over time.

Dr. Fennoy earned her Doctor of Medicine from the University of California, San Francisco School of Medicine. She completed pediatric training through the Department of Social Pediatrics at Montefiore Hospital in the Bronx. Social pediatrics considers not only disease but the family, community and environmental conditions that can influence a child's health. She later earned a Master of Public Health from Columbia University, adding a population-health perspective to her clinical work.

Nutrition became another major component of Dr. Fennoy's professional development. Columbia documents her training at the Institute of Human Nutrition at Columbia University. Nutrition and endocrinology are closely connected through metabolism, insulin function, growth, body composition and bone health. This additional preparation supported the multidisciplinary approach that characterizes her work in pediatric metabolic health.

Dr. Fennoy pursued pediatric endocrine training associated with the National Institutes of Health and NewYork-Presbyterian/Columbia University Medical Center. She is board certified in Pediatrics and Pediatric Endocrinology. Her clinical expertise includes growth disorders, thyroid disease, congenital adrenal hyperplasia, Cushing's syndrome, diabetes insipidus, type 2 diabetes, pituitary disorders, calcium-metabolism disorders, bone disease, Klinefelter syndrome and childhood and adolescent obesity.

Dr. Fennoy serves as Professor of Pediatrics at Columbia University Irving Medical Center and is part of the Division of Pediatric Endocrinology, Diabetes, and Metabolism. Academic medicine places patient care alongside research and education. Dr. Fennoy's work encompasses all three, allowing clinical questions to inform research while accumulated expertise helps educate future physicians and specialists.

A central theme in Dr. Fennoy's research is childhood and adolescent obesity. Her work seeks to better understand the medical problems associated with severe pediatric obesity and the factors that contribute to long-term morbidity. Rather than focusing on body weight alone, this research examines metabolic and physiological consequences that can include insulin resistance and cardiovascular, liver and skeletal concerns.

Dr. Fennoy has investigated relationships between insulin resistance and cardiovascular risk factors in developing children. She has also participated in school-based research evaluating nutrition education and exercise in relation to insulin resistance among middle-school students. This work connects pediatric endocrinology with prevention, nutrition and public-health education.

Dr. Fennoy serves as Medical Director of Columbia's Comprehensive Adolescent Bariatric Surgery Program. Her research has examined health changes in adolescents following bariatric surgery, including changes in obesity-related medical problems and bone health. Adolescent bariatric care is multidisciplinary and requires careful evaluation and long-term follow-up because patients are still developing physically and psychologically.

Columbia lists bone health, childhood obesity and Klinefelter syndrome among Dr. Fennoy's research interests. Her work includes questions involving increased body fat and low bone density. These issues illustrate how endocrinology connects hormones, metabolism, growth and skeletal health.

As Director of the POWER Program for Overweight Education and Reduction, Dr. Fennoy's responsibilities also reflect the importance of education in pediatric health. Weight management in children and adolescents can involve families, nutrition, physical activity, medical conditions and the broader social environment. Education helps patients and families understand both the health concerns being addressed and the reasons behind recommended interventions.

Dr. Fennoy's career demonstrates the value of bringing multiple disciplines together. Pediatrics provides a developmental perspective. Endocrinology provides specialized knowledge of hormones and metabolism. Nutrition addresses dietary and metabolic health. Public health considers prevention and population patterns. Research helps determine which interventions may be useful. Together, these fields create a broader approach to children's health.

Columbia identifies Dr. Fennoy as both a clinician and investigator. Her research has included pediatric endocrine clinical studies and investigations involving obesity, growth and metabolic health. Clinical research allows physicians to evaluate therapies, understand disease progression and study outcomes in defined patient populations while accounting for the special developmental considerations of children.

As a professor at Columbia, Dr. Fennoy contributes to an academic environment that trains medical students, residents, fellows and future physician-scientists. Experienced academic physicians can multiply their impact by passing clinical knowledge, research methods and professional judgment to future generations of specialists.

In 2024, Dr. Fennoy received the Outstanding Leadership in Endocrinology Award from the Endocrine Society. Columbia University Irving Medical Center and the Vagelos College of Physicians and Surgeons both documented the honor. The recognition highlights the leadership dimension of a career spanning clinical medicine, research, education and program development.

Columbia's Department of Pediatrics also identified Dr. Fennoy among faculty included in New York Magazine's 2024 Top Doctors list. Such recognition provides additional context for a career whose principal focus remains pediatric endocrine care, metabolic-health research and medical education.

Pediatric medicine can influence health far into adulthood. A metabolic condition identified in childhood may affect future cardiovascular, endocrine or metabolic health. Dr. Fennoy's research into obesity-related comorbidities therefore reflects a life-course perspective: understanding when and how interventions may help limit progression toward later disease.

Children do not manage health conditions in isolation. Parents, guardians, schools and communities can influence nutrition, physical activity and chronic-disease management. Pediatric obesity programs therefore frequently require sustained family involvement and multidisciplinary support. Dr. Fennoy's combined background in social pediatrics, nutrition, public health and endocrinology is well suited to these complex realities.

Many of the questions underlying Dr. Fennoy's work have direct clinical significance. When should clinicians intervene? Which young patients face greater metabolic risks? How does severe obesity affect other systems? What changes after bariatric surgery? How might nutrition education and physical activity influence metabolic health? These questions connect scientific measurement with the practical goal of improving care for children and adolescents.

Modern pediatric endocrinology crosses traditional boundaries. Specialists may need to understand metabolism, nutrition, public health, bone health, cardiovascular risk, growth and family behavior. Academic medicine adds research and teaching to those responsibilities. Leadership adds the challenge of creating programs capable of bringing multiple specialists together around patients' needs. Dr. Fennoy's professional journey incorporates each of these dimensions.

Dynamo Times recognizes Ilene Fennoy, MD, MPH, for her contributions to pediatric endocrinology, childhood-obesity research, nutrition, medical education and leadership in children's health.

About Columbia University Irving Medical Center

Ilene Fennoy, MD, MPH, is a board-certified pediatric endocrinologist and Professor of Pediatrics at Columbia University Irving Medical Center. She serves as Medical Director of the Comprehensive Adolescent Bariatric Surgery Program and Director of the POWER Program for Overweight Education and Reduction. Dr. Fennoy earned her MD from the University of California, San Francisco and her MPH from Columbia University. Her postgraduate training includes pediatrics at Montefiore Medical Center, nutrition training at Columbia's Institute of Human Nutrition, and pediatric endocrine training associated with NewYork-Presbyterian/Columbia and the National Institutes of Health. Her research interests include childhood obesity, bone health and Klinefelter syndrome.
