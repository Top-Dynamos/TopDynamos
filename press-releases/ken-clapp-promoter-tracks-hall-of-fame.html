Before the Boardroom, Ken Clapp Learned Racing by Promoting It Track by Track Across Northern California

Long before his years as a NASCAR executive, the Danville resident was running races at Vallejo, Altamont and Sears Point. Today he chairs the West Coast Stock Car Motorsports Hall of Fame, and the California Auto Racing Hall of Fame inducted him in 2026.

West Coast Stock Car Motorsports Hall of FameDanville, CANovember 16, 2026

DANVILLE, Calif. Most people who know Ken Clapp's name know him as the NASCAR executive who helped bring big league stock car racing to the West. Fewer know how he learned the business: by renting tracks, selling tickets and counting the crowd himself.

Born Charles Kendall Clapp in Oakland in 1939, he saw his first race on October 7, 1951, at the San Joaquin County Fairgrounds, introduced to the sport by a local racer named Fred Erickson. Within a few years he was turning wrenches for car owner Cos Cancilla and working on the team of Danny Letner, who won NASCAR's Pacific Coast Grand National title in 1955.

His first promotion came in 1966. With Gary Gorman, a classmate from Acalanes High School, he began promoting NASCAR Pacific Coast Late Model races at Vallejo Speedway. The first show, on July 31, 1966, drew about 3,700 people. The pair went on to run races at Altamont Speedway, Shasta Speedway and the Alameda County Fairgrounds until 1968.

That same year he leased Sears Point, the road course that later became Sonoma Raceway, for USAC and NASCAR events, and in October 1969 he became the track's vice president and public relations director. Not every venture worked. Looking back on a short run managing Altamont Speedway in 1974, he told the San Francisco Chronicle's SFGate in 2009, "For every home run, there were 99 failures."

The lessons carried into his NASCAR years. He worked alongside Bill France Jr. and Les Richter on plans for western tracks, and when an effort to build a Cup Series track at the Santa Clara County Fairgrounds in San Jose fell through in the late 1980s, the Cup dates from Riverside went to Sonoma and Phoenix instead. In the mid 1990s he served on the board of Las Vegas Motor Speedway and later helped secure the track's first Cup Series date, run in 1998.

Hollywood called on his knowledge as well. He served as a racing consultant on the television film Steel Chariots in 1997 and on the feature films Herbie: Fully Loaded in 2005 and Talladega Nights in 2006.

His NASCAR career was matched by service across the sport. Starting with the class of 2010 he was a voter for the NASCAR Hall of Fame, and in 2010 he was elected to the board of the National Hot Rod Association. He later consulted for Iowa Speedway after NASCAR acquired it.

Recognition followed him. The Motor Sports Press Association named him Promoter of the Year in 1980 and inducted him into its hall of fame in 2006. Sonoma Raceway added him to its Wall of Fame in 2008, and in 2026 he was inducted into the California Auto Racing Hall of Fame.

Since its founding in 2001 he has chaired the West Coast Stock Car Motorsports Hall of Fame, which inducted him in its first class in 2002. The hall holds its induction banquet each June at Sonoma Raceway, and its memorabilia is on display at a museum in Paso Robles.

He has called his 2022 book, Sea to Shining Sea: Racing from the Wild West to Daytona, an effort to restore the history of racing in the West. By his own count in the book, his career has touched a bit more than 6,000 single day racing events.

Dynamo Times recognizes Ken Clapp for building western racing from the grandstands up, and for keeping its history alive for the next generation of fans.

About West Coast Stock Car Motorsports Hall of Fame

Ken Clapp is a retired NASCAR senior vice president and the chairman of the West Coast Stock Car Motorsports Hall of Fame. Before joining NASCAR he promoted races at Vallejo, Altamont and Sears Point in Northern California. He is a member of several motorsports halls of fame, including the California Auto Racing Hall of Fame, which inducted him in 2026.
