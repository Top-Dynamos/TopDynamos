Lawrence Steven Cosentino Brings Naval Nuclear Experience, Technical Expertise and Continuous Improvement to Risk Control

Risk control consultant and U.S. Navy veteran Lawrence Steven Cosentino has built a multidisciplinary career spanning nuclear electrical systems, emergency response, loss control, quality assurance, insurance risk evaluation and process improvement.

TravelersOssining, New YorkAugust 28, 2026

OSSINING, N.Y.: Dynamo Times highlights Lawrence Steven Cosentino, a risk control professional and United States Navy veteran whose career has developed at the intersection of technical systems, quality assurance, inspection, safety and insurance risk management.

Cosentino has served as a risk control consultant with Travelers since 2022. His work involves evaluating client properties and equipment for potential liabilities and insurance risks and communicating field findings that support informed risk decisions. His path into the field is distinctive. Before his civilian work in risk control, Cosentino served in the United States Navy from 2006 to 2014 as a nuclear electrician, gaining experience with sophisticated steam-powered and electrical systems aboard nuclear submarines. That highly technical environment helped establish a foundation in inspection, quality assurance, procedural discipline and operational safety that he later carried into civilian industry.

Risk control requires the ability to identify problems before they become larger losses. The work can involve examining physical properties, equipment, operating practices and safety procedures while translating technical observations into information that organizations and insurers can use. Cosentino's career has progressively moved through technical and operational positions toward specialized risk-control consulting. His professional history demonstrates how engineering-oriented experience, inspection knowledge, analytical thinking and communication can converge within the insurance and risk-management field.

From 2006 through 2014, Cosentino served in the United States Navy as a nuclear electrician. His service included work aboard nuclear submarines, including the USS Jacksonville and later the USS Missouri. Nuclear propulsion environments demand attention to procedure, equipment reliability, quality assurance and safety. Cosentino has credited his Navy experience with helping him develop an open-minded approach to decision-making rather than operating automatically from habit. That mindset would become valuable later in his civilian career, where risk professionals must continually evaluate changing conditions instead of assuming that an established practice is necessarily the safest or most efficient approach.

Leaving military service required translating highly specialized technical skills into civilian applications. Cosentino's experience with electrical and steam-powered systems provided a foundation relevant to equipment inspection, maintenance, emergency response and loss prevention. After his military career, he held technical and operational roles that broadened his understanding of industrial environments. His professional history includes work as a senior technician on an emergency response team at GlobalFoundries and as a maintenance supervisor for Gourmet Boutique. These roles expanded his experience with operational reliability and workplace risk.

Cosentino later moved deeper into the insurance and inspection field through work with Hartford Steam Boiler Inspection and Insurance Company. His responsibilities included technical and engineering-related work connected with loss control. Loss-control professionals examine conditions that could lead to equipment failure, property damage, injury or other insured losses. The objective is preventive. Rather than responding only after an incident occurs, effective risk control attempts to identify vulnerabilities early and provide information that can help organizations address them. Cosentino's background with complex equipment made this field a natural extension of his earlier technical experience.

During his work with Hartford Steam Boiler, Cosentino received an Award for Exemplary Integrity in 2019. Integrity is particularly important in inspection and risk-control work. Clients, insurers and regulatory authorities may rely on the accuracy of a professional's observations. A risk-control consultant must therefore be willing to document conditions objectively, communicate concerns clearly and maintain professional standards even when findings may be inconvenient. The recognition became an important milestone in Cosentino's developing civilian career.

Since 2022, Cosentino has worked as a risk control consultant at Travelers. His responsibilities include evaluating client properties and equipment for liabilities and insurance risks through fieldwork. This role brings together several elements of his professional background. His technical experience helps him understand equipment and operating systems. His inspection experience supports systematic evaluation. His quality-assurance background encourages attention to procedures and standards. His analytical training supports the interpretation of findings. Together, those skills help him identify risk exposures and communicate information relevant to insurance and operational decision-making.

Risk control is not simply about confirming that procedures exist. It can also involve asking whether existing procedures still make sense. A Marquis Millennium profile of Cosentino describes his work reviewing outdated practices and reports that these efforts helped avoid substantial city-mandated inspection expenses. The example reflects a broader principle of continuous improvement: a process should not be retained solely because it has always been done that way. Professionals must be willing to examine requirements, identify inefficiencies and determine whether a safer or more effective approach is available.

Cosentino holds Lean Six Sigma Green Belt and Black Belt certifications from Villanova University. Lean Six Sigma methodologies emphasize process improvement, reduction of waste and variation, measurement and data-informed decision-making. These principles align naturally with risk-control work. A recurring operational problem may signal a process weakness. Repeated inspection findings may indicate a systemic issue. Unnecessary procedures may consume time and resources without improving safety. Continuous-improvement methods provide structured ways to examine those problems and develop more efficient processes.

Cosentino earned a Bachelor of Science in Computer Information Systems from Manhattan College, now Manhattan University, in 2005. Information systems education develops skills involving technology, data and organizational processes. Although his later career became heavily focused on physical systems and risk control, the analytical framework of information systems remains relevant. Modern risk professionals increasingly work with digital records, data analysis, reporting platforms and technology-enabled inspection processes. Cosentino's educational and technical background therefore spans both information technology and physical systems.

Professional development has remained an important theme throughout Cosentino's career. He has been pursuing a Master of Business Administration with a concentration in Business Analytics. Business analytics provides tools for examining organizational data and translating information into decisions. Within risk management, analytical capabilities can complement field experience by helping professionals recognize patterns, quantify problems and evaluate operational performance. The combination of hands-on technical knowledge and business analytics represents a broader evolution from technician to strategic risk professional.

Although Cosentino has worked in different industries, quality assurance is a recurring theme throughout his career. In the Navy, strict quality standards were essential when working around nuclear propulsion systems. In industrial environments, equipment reliability and maintenance required careful procedures. In loss control, inspections helped identify potential failures and hazards. In risk consulting, the same mindset is applied to client properties, equipment and operating practices. The setting changed, but the underlying professional discipline remained consistent.

Risk management often becomes visible only after something goes wrong. Effective risk control works in the opposite direction. Its success can be measured by problems that never happen. A hazard identified before an accident, an equipment issue corrected before failure or an inefficient procedure revised before unnecessary costs accumulate can all represent meaningful outcomes. Cosentino's work is centered on this preventive approach. The objective is to identify exposures, evaluate their significance and provide information that supports safer and more effective operations.

Cosentino's commitment to service extends beyond his professional career. In 2022, he was commissioned as a deacon by Village Lutheran Church in Bronxville, New York. His community involvement has also included environmental volunteer activity and participation in veterans organizations. Professional sources identify involvement with VFW Post 2285, American Legion Post 979 and the Eastchester Veterans Foundation. These activities continue a service-oriented dimension of his life that began during his years in the Navy.

Leadership in technical fields often begins with credibility. Professionals must understand the systems they evaluate before others will trust their recommendations. Cosentino's career combines practical experience with formal education and continuing professional development. His background includes nuclear electrical work, emergency response, maintenance supervision, equipment inspection, insurance loss control, Lean Six Sigma and risk consulting. This breadth allows him to approach problems from more than one perspective.

In July 2025, The Inner Circle acknowledged Cosentino as a Pinnacle Professional Member for his contributions to quality assurance and risk control. A later recognition identified him as a Pinnacle Professional Member Inner Circle of Excellence. He has also been featured by Marquis Millennium for his work in risk control and professional development. These profiles highlight a career built through technical expertise, military service, continuous education and a commitment to improving the systems and organizations around him.

Risk control will continue to evolve as businesses adopt new technologies, equipment becomes increasingly connected and organizations rely more heavily on data to understand operational exposure. Professionals in the field will need both practical knowledge and analytical capabilities. Cosentino's background brings those areas together. His career began with demanding technical responsibilities in the United States Navy and developed through industrial operations, loss control, inspection, continuous improvement and insurance risk consulting.

Dynamo Times recognizes Lawrence Steven Cosentino for his contributions to quality assurance, risk control, operational improvement, technical leadership and community service.

About Travelers

Lawrence Steven Cosentino is a risk control consultant and United States Navy veteran based in Ossining, New York. He has worked as a risk control consultant at Travelers since 2022, evaluating properties and equipment for liability and insurance risks. Cosentino previously worked in loss control with Hartford Steam Boiler Inspection and Insurance Company and held technical and operational positions with Gourmet Boutique and GlobalFoundries. He served in the United States Navy from 2006 to 2014 as a nuclear electrician aboard nuclear submarines including the USS Jacksonville and USS Missouri. He earned a Bachelor of Science in Computer Information Systems from Manhattan College and holds Lean Six Sigma Green Belt and Black Belt certifications from Villanova University.
