Donald Massenburg, MD, PhD, FACP, FACR, Advances Rheumatology Through Patient Education, Scientific Insight and Global Medical Service

Board-certified rheumatologist Donald Massenburg, MD, PhD, FACP, FACR, has built a career combining complex autoimmune and musculoskeletal care, scientific training, patient education and decades of international medical service.

Prevea HealthGreen Bay, WisconsinAugust 31, 2026

GREEN BAY, Wis.: Dynamo Times highlights Donald Massenburg, MD, PhD, FACP, FACR, a board-certified rheumatologist whose professional life brings together scientific inquiry, complex clinical medicine, patient education and international service.

Dr. Massenburg treats patients with autoimmune, inflammatory and musculoskeletal conditions including rheumatoid arthritis, psoriatic arthritis, lupus, vasculitis, gout, polymyalgia rheumatica, ankylosing spondylitis, scleroderma and other rheumatic diseases. His clinical philosophy emphasizes asking questions, listening carefully and helping patients understand the conditions affecting them. That approach is supported by an unusually deep scientific foundation: a chemistry degree from Haverford College, a PhD in biochemistry and biophysics from the University of North Carolina at Chapel Hill and an MD from Duke University School of Medicine. In 2026, The Inner Circle recognized Dr. Massenburg as a 2026 Leader in Rheumatology for his contributions to rheumatology and global medical service.

Rheumatology frequently requires physicians to solve difficult diagnostic problems. Many rheumatic diseases can affect multiple organ systems. Symptoms may overlap, change over time or resemble other medical conditions. Dr. Massenburg's background in both scientific research and clinical medicine provides a framework for approaching that complexity. His education began with chemistry and advanced into biochemistry and biophysics before he entered medical school. That progression reflects a career shaped by a desire to understand disease from both the molecular and human perspectives.

Dr. Massenburg earned his Bachelor of Science in Chemistry from Haverford College. He then pursued doctoral study at the University of North Carolina at Chapel Hill, earning a PhD in Biochemistry and Biophysics. Biochemistry and biophysics examine fundamental processes that underlie living systems. For a future physician, this training provided an extensive scientific foundation for understanding cellular behavior, molecular interactions and disease mechanisms. That scientific background would later complement his work in immunology and rheumatology.

Dr. Massenburg earned his Doctor of Medicine from Duke University School of Medicine, completing medical school in 1997. His medical education transformed his scientific background into a clinical career centered on diagnosing and treating patients. He subsequently completed an internal medicine residency at Advocate Lutheran General Hospital in Park Ridge, Illinois. Internal medicine training provides broad experience managing adult disease and is the foundation required for advanced fellowship training in rheumatology.

After completing his internal medicine residency, Dr. Massenburg pursued a rheumatology fellowship at the University of Chicago. Rheumatology focuses on diseases affecting joints, muscles, connective tissues and the immune system. Many of these disorders are autoimmune, meaning the body's immune system becomes involved in damaging its own tissues. The specialty requires physicians to combine clinical examination, laboratory findings, imaging and detailed patient histories. Dr. Massenburg became board certified in rheumatology and is a Fellow of both the American College of Rheumatology and the American College of Physicians.

Dr. Massenburg's clinical practice encompasses a broad range of rheumatic and autoimmune conditions. His current professional profile at Prevea Health identifies services involving rheumatoid arthritis, juvenile idiopathic arthritis, psoriasis, psoriatic arthritis, inflammatory eye disorders, lupus, gout, polymyalgia rheumatica, ankylosing spondylitis, polymyositis, dermatomyositis, sarcoidosis and scleroderma. These conditions vary considerably: some primarily affect joints, others can involve skin, muscles, blood vessels, eyes or internal organs. Managing them requires a physician to understand the whole patient rather than focusing only on one symptom.

One of the strongest themes in Dr. Massenburg's professional philosophy is education. A chronic autoimmune condition may require long-term monitoring and treatment. Patients therefore benefit from understanding what their disease is, why symptoms occur and how medications or other treatments are intended to help. Dr. Massenburg emphasizes asking and answering questions as a way to learn about patients and build relationships with them. This approach turns the medical visit into a collaborative process where the patient contributes personal experience and symptoms and the physician contributes clinical knowledge and diagnostic reasoning.

Advanced technology plays an important role in modern medicine, but patient communication remains fundamental. A laboratory result can provide data. Imaging can reveal structural changes. Yet neither can replace a detailed conversation about what a patient is experiencing. Dr. Massenburg's clinical philosophy places substantial value on listening. This can be particularly important in rheumatology because symptoms may evolve over time and may not always be visible during a single examination. Understanding how symptoms affect a patient's daily life can provide important context for diagnosis and treatment.

Dr. Massenburg's scientific background extends beyond his academic degrees. Publicly indexed medical literature identifies him as a contributor to research involving immune signaling, cellular responses and biological mechanisms. His publications include work related to B-cell antigen receptor signaling and cellular responses involving apoptotic cells. Research experience strengthens the connection between laboratory science and clinical medicine. For rheumatologists, that connection is particularly relevant because many modern therapies target specific components of the immune system.

Rheumatology and immunology are closely connected. Autoimmune disease develops when immune processes that normally protect the body become misdirected. Understanding these disorders requires knowledge of immune cells, inflammatory pathways and signaling mechanisms. Dr. Massenburg's background in biochemistry, biophysics and medical research provides additional perspective when evaluating diseases driven by immune dysfunction. This scientific foundation complements his clinical experience treating patients whose conditions can involve multiple biological systems.

Dr. Massenburg's career extends well beyond his clinical practice in Wisconsin. He has served as Medical Director for International/American Medical Missions for approximately 25 years. Through this work, he has volunteered medical expertise in underserved communities outside the continental United States. Professional profiles document service in Jamaica, Haiti, the Dominican Republic, the Bahamas, Puerto Rico and Belize. This long-term commitment to medical missions represents another dimension of his professional life.

Delivering medical care in underserved communities can require physicians to work with different resources, health systems and patient populations. The experience can also reinforce a fundamental reality: chronic disease and the need for medical care cross geographic and economic boundaries. Dr. Massenburg's international service has allowed him to apply his medical knowledge in communities where specialty resources may be limited. His role as medical director adds a leadership component to that service, combining clinical responsibility with organization and coordination.

Dr. Massenburg holds the professional distinctions FACP and FACR, reflecting fellowship in the American College of Physicians and American College of Rheumatology. He is also affiliated with the National Medical Association and the Wisconsin Rheumatology Association. Professional organizations provide physicians with opportunities for continuing education, scientific exchange and collaboration. In a specialty where treatments and scientific understanding continue to evolve, maintaining connections with the broader medical community supports ongoing professional development.

Dr. Massenburg has publicly credited several mentors who influenced different stages of his professional development. The 2026 recognition profile identifies Claude Wintner, PhD; Barry Lentz, PhD; Barton Haynes, MD; Marc Fine, MD; Marcus Clark, MD; and Alvin Wells, MD, PhD among those who helped shape his approach to science and medicine. Mentorship can have lasting influence in a medical career: scientific mentors teach how to ask meaningful questions, clinical mentors demonstrate how to care for patients, and professional mentors help physicians understand how to develop their own approach to leadership and service.

Current provider information identifies Dr. Massenburg as a rheumatologist with Prevea Health in Green Bay, Wisconsin. His practice focuses on diagnosing and treating a wide range of rheumatic and autoimmune conditions. His work continues to combine clinical problem-solving with a strong emphasis on patient relationships and education.

In November 2025, The Inner Circle acknowledged Dr. Massenburg as a Pinnacle Lifetime Member for contributions to rheumatology and global medical service. In May 2026, the organization recognized him as a 2026 Leader in Rheumatology. These recognitions highlight both sides of his career: specialty medical care and service to communities beyond his primary practice.

Looking forward, Dr. Massenburg has expressed an interest in expanding public awareness and education surrounding rheumatic diseases. This goal aligns naturally with his patient-centered approach. Greater awareness can help people recognize symptoms, understand why specialist evaluation may be needed and become more informed participants in their own care. For chronic illnesses, education can remain valuable long after the initial diagnosis.

Dr. Massenburg's professional journey illustrates several dimensions of medicine. He is a scientist by training, a physician by profession, an educator in his relationships with patients and a volunteer and medical leader through international missions. These roles reinforce one another: scientific knowledge informs diagnosis, clinical experience generates new questions, education helps patients participate in treatment and service expands the reach of medical expertise.

Dynamo Times recognizes Donald Massenburg, MD, PhD, FACP, FACR, for his contributions to rheumatology, patient education, scientific medicine and global medical service.

About Prevea Health

Donald Massenburg, MD, PhD, FACP, FACR, is a board-certified rheumatologist practicing with Prevea Health in Green Bay, Wisconsin. He earned a Bachelor of Science in Chemistry from Haverford College, a PhD in Biochemistry and Biophysics from the University of North Carolina at Chapel Hill and his MD from Duke University School of Medicine. He completed an internal medicine residency at Advocate Lutheran General Hospital and a rheumatology fellowship at the University of Chicago. Dr. Massenburg is a Fellow of the American College of Rheumatology and American College of Physicians and has served as Medical Director for International/American Medical Missions for approximately 25 years.
