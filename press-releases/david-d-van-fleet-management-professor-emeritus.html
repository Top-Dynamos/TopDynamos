David D. Van Fleet, PhD, Builds a Six-Decade Legacy in Management Education, Research and Leadership

Arizona State University Professor Emeritus David D. Van Fleet, PhD, has spent more than half a century teaching, researching and writing about management, organizational behavior, leadership, agribusiness, workplace violence and the history of management thought.

Arizona State UniversitySanta Clarita, CASeptember 22, 2026

Dynamo Times highlights David D. Van Fleet, PhD, Professor Emeritus at Arizona State University and a longtime management scholar whose academic career has spanned teaching, research, professional leadership, publishing and consulting.

The Inner Circle recognizes Dr. Van Fleet as a Pinnacle Lifetime Achiever for contributions to higher education and management scholarship. Arizona State University's official profile confirms that he joined ASU in 1989 and identifies his research interests as the history of management thought, leadership and military leadership, management in agribusiness, workplace violence, managerial bullying and harassment, and terrorism. His career before ASU included positions at Texas A&M University, the University of Akron and the University of Tennessee.

Dr. Van Fleet's professional life has centered on understanding organizations and the people who lead and work within them. Management is a broad discipline. It encompasses leadership, decision-making, organizational behavior, workplace relationships, strategy, history and the ways institutions respond to change. Over the course of his career, Dr. Van Fleet has explored many of these areas through both teaching and research.

Dr. Van Fleet earned both his undergraduate and doctoral degrees from the University of Tennessee, Knoxville. Arizona State University lists his BS as having been completed in 1962 and his PhD in 1969. His curriculum vitae describes his doctoral work as involving economics and management. His dissertation examined decision-making in the firm through a case study of Kingsport Press covering the period from 1927 to 1967.

Dr. Van Fleet's academic career began at the University of Tennessee. His professional curriculum vitae describes more than 50 years of full-time teaching experience between 1963 and 2019. During that period, he taught dozens of different courses at both undergraduate and graduate levels. The scale of that career means his work influenced not only scholarship but generations of students entering business, management and related professions.

After completing his doctorate, Dr. Van Fleet joined the University of Akron. Arizona State University's biography identifies Akron as one of the institutions where he served before joining ASU. His academic path would later take him to Texas A&M University and ultimately Arizona State University. Each institution added another stage to a career increasingly focused on organizational behavior, management research and professional service.

Dr. Van Fleet spent a substantial portion of his career at Texas A&M University. His professional record identifies him as a professor there from the 1970s through 1989. In 1985, he received a Sustained Outstanding Service Award from Texas A&M's College of Business Administration. Service in academic life can include committee leadership, professional organizations, program development, mentoring and contributions to the wider university community.

In 1989, Dr. Van Fleet joined Arizona State University. ASU's official faculty profile currently identifies him as Professor Emeritus with the Morrison School of Agribusiness. His ASU career included work on the university's West and Polytechnic campuses and responsibilities extending beyond classroom teaching. He served as MBA director at ASU West from 2000 through 2004 and participated in numerous university committees and academic initiatives.

One dimension of Dr. Van Fleet's research involves management in agribusiness. Agribusiness combines traditional management challenges with the distinctive realities of agriculture, food production, supply chains and commodity markets. His later academic affiliation with ASU's Morrison School of Agribusiness provided an environment in which management scholarship could be applied to this specialized sector.

Dr. Van Fleet has maintained a longstanding interest in the history of management thought. Studying management historically helps scholars understand how ideas about organizations, supervision, leadership and employee behavior developed. Concepts that appear modern often have much older roots. Historical research can also reveal why certain management ideas gained influence and how they changed as workplaces evolved.

Leadership is another major theme in Dr. Van Fleet's scholarship. His ASU biography specifically identifies both leadership and military leadership among his research interests. Military organizations provide distinctive settings for studying authority, hierarchy, coordination, decision-making and behavior under pressure. Comparing military and civilian organizations can help researchers examine which leadership behaviors are broadly applicable and which depend on organizational context.

Organizational behavior examines how individuals and groups act within institutions. It includes questions involving motivation, leadership, conflict, communication, power and workplace culture. Dr. Van Fleet's publication history includes extensive work in these areas. His scholarship has helped connect traditional management theory with emerging workplace concerns.

Dr. Van Fleet's research also addresses workplace violence. This subject requires organizations to think beyond productivity and efficiency to employee safety, prevention, organizational climate and risk management. ASU identifies workplace violence, particularly within agribusiness, as one of his research interests. His later work has continued to examine how organizations can understand and respond to harmful workplace behavior.

Managerial bullying and harassment are additional areas of Dr. Van Fleet's research. A 2018 article co-authored with colleagues examined the meanings of bullying and harassment in organizational settings. Research in this area helps clarify terminology and encourages organizations to examine behaviors that may damage employees, teams and institutional performance. It also illustrates how management scholarship increasingly addresses the quality and safety of workplace relationships.

ASU's official biography also lists terrorism among Dr. Van Fleet's research interests. Although the topic may appear distant from traditional management, organizations must sometimes plan for threats, crises and extreme disruptions. Management research can contribute to understanding organizational preparedness, leadership and response in high-risk environments.

The Inner Circle profile states that Dr. Van Fleet has authored more than a dozen books. His curriculum vitae provides greater detail, documenting a large body of publications, presentations and research produced over the course of his career. His books and scholarly work have addressed management, organizational behavior and related areas. The breadth of the record reflects decades of sustained academic activity.

Dr. Van Fleet's curriculum vitae reports more than 400 publications, presentations and research contributions, including books, refereed journal articles, invited chapters, technical reports, proceedings and professional presentations. Because totals can change as new work is added and because different profiles count categories differently, Dynamo Times treats this as a figure reported in his professional CV rather than an independently audited bibliometric total. The larger point is clear: his scholarly output extends across many decades.

Dr. Van Fleet has also contributed to academic publishing through editorial leadership. ASU records his service as editor of the Journal of Management from 1987 to 1989 and the Journal of Behavioral and Applied Management from 2006 to 2008. Editors play an important role in scholarly communication. They help guide peer review, evaluate research and shape the conversations taking place within an academic field.

Dr. Van Fleet has maintained extensive involvement with the Academy of Management. ASU lists him as a Fellow of the Academy of Management beginning in 1985. In 2008, he received the organization's Distinguished Service Award. The award reflects a dimension of academic life beyond publishing: sustained service to the institutions that support a professional field.

The Southern Management Association has also played an important role in Dr. Van Fleet's professional service. The Inner Circle profile notes his leadership within the Southwest Division of Management. ASU records that he became a Fellow of the Southern Management Association in 1995 and received its Sustained Outstanding Service Award in 2005. These roles reflect decades of involvement in the professional management community.

The Inner Circle specifically highlights Dr. Van Fleet's receipt of the Ronald G. Greenwood Award. His professional curriculum vitae identifies this as a lifetime-achievement recognition from the Management History Division of the Academy of Management in 2023. The honor aligns closely with one of his principal research interests: the history of management thought.

Dr. Van Fleet's work has extended beyond academia. ASU lists consulting clients including the American Cancer Society, Kuwait Oil Company, State of Kuwait, Kuwait University, Shell Oil, the Community Council of Greater Dallas and several Texas municipalities. Consulting provides an opportunity to apply academic knowledge to organizational problems outside the classroom. It can also expose researchers to practical challenges that later inform teaching and scholarship.

His curriculum vitae describes participation in more than 150 executive-education programs. Executive education differs from traditional degree programs because participants often bring substantial workplace experience into the classroom. Teaching these audiences requires connecting theory directly to management practice. Dr. Van Fleet's extensive involvement demonstrates the applied side of his academic career.

Dr. Van Fleet received an ASU Faculty Achievement Award in Service for the 2004-2005 academic year. ASU also records extensive committee and university service. Academic institutions depend on faculty not only to teach and publish but also to participate in governance, hiring, promotion, curriculum development and program review. These responsibilities formed a substantial part of his professional record.

A career extending from the 1960s into the twenty-first century provides a distinctive perspective on the evolution of management. Organizations became more global. Technology transformed communication. Workplaces became increasingly digital. Management scholarship expanded its attention to culture, harassment, violence, ethics and employee well-being. Dr. Van Fleet's research interests evolved alongside these changes while maintaining a strong connection to the history of the field.

The Inner Circle profile states that Dr. Van Fleet continues to focus on writing and completing additional book work. Research profiles also show scholarly activity extending well beyond his formal retirement from full-time teaching. Professor emeritus status can provide scholars with an opportunity to continue research and writing without the same classroom and administrative responsibilities carried during full-time faculty service.

Dr. Van Fleet's career combines several dimensions of academic life. He taught thousands of students. He published books and articles. He edited scholarly journals. He served professional associations. He advised organizations. He helped administer academic programs. And he continued studying how management ideas and workplace behavior change over time.

Dynamo Times recognizes David D. Van Fleet, PhD, for a six-decade legacy in management education, organizational research, professional service and leadership.

About Arizona State University

David D. Van Fleet, PhD, is Professor Emeritus with Arizona State University's Morrison School of Agribusiness. He earned a BS from the University of Tennessee in 1962 and a PhD from the University of Tennessee in 1969. His research interests include the history of management thought, leadership and military leadership, management in agribusiness, workplace violence, managerial bullying and harassment, and terrorism. Before joining Arizona State University in 1989, he held academic positions at Texas A&M University, the University of Akron and the University of Tennessee. His honors include the Academy of Management Distinguished Service Award, Southern Management Association Sustained Outstanding Service Award and the Management History Division's Ronald G. Greenwood Award.
