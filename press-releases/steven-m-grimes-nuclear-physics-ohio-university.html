Steven M. Grimes, PhD, Builds a Distinguished Legacy in Nuclear Physics, Research and Scientific Education

Ohio University Distinguished Professor Emeritus Steven M. Grimes has built a decades-long career spanning nuclear physics, accelerator-based research, scientific education and work with major U.S. national laboratories.

Ohio UniversityAthens, OhioAugust 31, 2026

ATHENS, Ohio: Dynamo Times highlights Steven M. Grimes, PhD, a nuclear physicist, researcher and longtime educator whose professional career has connected university scholarship, accelerator science and national laboratory research.

Dr. Grimes is a Distinguished Professor Emeritus at Ohio University. His career has included work at Lawrence Livermore National Laboratory, leadership of Ohio University's Edwards Accelerator Laboratory, decades of university teaching and research, and continued scientific collaboration. His research has contributed to the study of nuclear reactions, neutron physics and related areas of nuclear science. In November 2025, a Marquis Who's Who announcement highlighted Dr. Grimes and his earlier feature in the October/November 2022 Ohio regional issue of Forbes magazine.

Dr. Grimes's interest in science developed early. According to his professional profile, his father was an engineer, and conversations surrounding the design of the family home introduced him to practical questions involving heating, cooling and electrical systems. Those experiences helped cultivate an interest in understanding how physical systems work. What began as curiosity ultimately developed into a lifelong career in physics.

Dr. Grimes earned a Bachelor of Science from Stanford University in 1963. Stanford provided the first major academic foundation for the career that followed. He then continued his education at the University of Wisconsin, where his focus advanced from undergraduate physics into graduate-level scientific research.

Dr. Grimes earned a Master of Science from the University of Wisconsin in 1964 and a Doctor of Philosophy in 1968. Graduate training prepared him for advanced research in nuclear physics. During this period, he also became associated with the academic honor societies Sigma Xi and Phi Beta Kappa. His doctoral education opened the door to international and national-laboratory research opportunities.

Following his doctorate, Dr. Grimes spent a year as a postdoctoral fellow at the University of Basel in Switzerland. International postdoctoral work provided an opportunity to develop as an independent scientist while working within a different research environment. The experience was followed by another important stage of his career at one of the United States' major national laboratories.

Dr. Grimes completed an additional year of postdoctoral work at Lawrence Livermore National Laboratory in California. He subsequently joined the laboratory as a physicist and remained there from 1971 to 1981. National laboratories occupy an important position in American scientific research, bringing together specialized facilities and multidisciplinary teams to address complex scientific and technical questions. His decade at Lawrence Livermore strengthened a research foundation that he would later bring into academia.

During the later years of his Lawrence Livermore tenure, Dr. Grimes began teaching as a visiting professor at Ohio University in 1978. The experience developed into a permanent academic career. In 1981, he joined Ohio University full time as a professor of physics. The transition allowed him to combine research with another important responsibility: educating future scientists.

At Ohio University, Dr. Grimes became closely associated with the Edwards Accelerator Laboratory. The laboratory supports experimental research in areas including nuclear structure, nuclear astrophysics and applied nuclear physics. Dr. Grimes served as a researcher and director of the Edwards Accelerator Laboratory through 1991. His leadership placed him at the center of an environment where faculty, students and collaborators could investigate nuclear processes using accelerator-based experimental methods.

Ohio University's Edwards Accelerator Laboratory includes a 4.5-MV tandem accelerator and specialized equipment for producing and studying nuclear reactions. The facility has supported research involving neutron production and measurement, nuclear structure, astrophysics and applied nuclear physics. Ohio University's own technical materials continue to cite Dr. Grimes as a contributor to the laboratory's neutron-source capabilities. His association with the facility represents one of the most significant institutional threads in his academic career.

Neutrons play a central role in many areas of nuclear science. Understanding neutron interactions is important to fundamental nuclear physics as well as applications involving nuclear energy, astrophysics, detector calibration and other scientific fields. Dr. Grimes has contributed to research involving neutron production, neutron scattering, nuclear reactions and experimental measurement. His work appears across decades of scientific literature and reflects sustained involvement in experimental nuclear physics.

Dr. Grimes has contributed expertise to numerous scientific and professional publications. His published work includes studies involving nuclear level densities, neutron-nucleus interactions, fission neutron spectra and neutron-scattering measurements. A 2021 paper in Annals of Nuclear Energy, co-authored with Nikolay V. Kornilov, examined prompt fission neutron spectra and the continuing challenge of reconciling microscopic and macroscopic experimental data. His publication history demonstrates that his research involvement continued well beyond the early decades of his career.

Scientific progress depends not only on conducting experiments but also on organizing and communicating knowledge. Dr. Grimes served as an editor for "Moment Methods" in 1979 and "Neutron-Nucleus Collisions" in 1985. Editorial work provides another form of contribution to a scientific discipline: it helps researchers organize findings, clarify technical discussions and make specialized knowledge more useful to the broader scientific community.

Ohio University named Dr. Grimes a Distinguished Professor in 2001. The designation marked an important milestone in a career that combined research, teaching and scientific leadership. He later became a Distinguished Professor Emeritus in 2011. Emeritus status recognized his longstanding contributions while allowing his relationship with the university and scientific community to continue.

Dr. Grimes's professional relationship with national laboratories did not end when he entered academia. His Marquis profile reports that he has consulted for Lawrence Livermore National Laboratory and Los Alamos National Laboratory. These continuing connections reflect the value of long-term expertise in specialized areas of nuclear science and demonstrate how academic and national-laboratory research communities frequently overlap through collaboration, consultation and shared scientific objectives.

Nuclear astrophysics examines how nuclear processes influence stars, stellar explosions and the creation of elements. It sits at the intersection of nuclear physics and astronomy. Research facilities such as the Edwards Accelerator Laboratory contribute to this field by enabling scientists to study nuclear reactions under controlled laboratory conditions. Dr. Grimes's professional recognition has highlighted nuclear astrophysics as an important dimension of his expertise.

Dr. Grimes is a Fellow of the American Physical Society. APS Fellowship recognizes members who have made advances in physics through original research, innovative contributions, application of physics, or significant contributions to physics education and service. The distinction places Dr. Grimes within a community of physicists recognized for significant professional contributions.

In 2008, Dr. Grimes was recognized as an Outstanding Referee. Peer review is an essential part of scientific publishing: referees evaluate research before publication, examining methodology, interpretation and scientific significance. The work is often performed behind the scenes, but it helps maintain standards within the scientific literature. Recognition for peer-review service therefore represents another dimension of Dr. Grimes's contribution to physics.

Marquis Who's Who selected Dr. Grimes for a feature appearing in the October/November 2022 Ohio regional issue of Forbes magazine. The feature highlighted his professional achievements and expertise. A November 2025 Marquis announcement revisited that recognition and summarized his career in nuclear physics, astrophysics, research and education.

The arc of Dr. Grimes's career stretches from early conversations about engineering systems to advanced nuclear research. Across that timeline, curiosity has remained a consistent theme. Physics asks fundamental questions about matter, energy and the forces governing physical systems. For Dr. Grimes, those questions developed into a career spanning research laboratories, classrooms, accelerator facilities and scientific publications.

Dynamo Times recognizes Steven M. Grimes, PhD, for his distinguished contributions to nuclear physics, nuclear astrophysics, scientific research, education and the advancement of experimental nuclear science.

About Ohio University

Steven M. Grimes, PhD, is a Distinguished Professor Emeritus of physics at Ohio University and a nuclear physicist whose career has included research, teaching and national-laboratory work. He earned a BS from Stanford University in 1963, an MS from the University of Wisconsin in 1964 and a PhD from the University of Wisconsin in 1968. After postdoctoral appointments at the University of Basel and Lawrence Livermore National Laboratory, he served as a physicist at Lawrence Livermore from 1971 to 1981. Dr. Grimes began teaching at Ohio University as a visiting professor in 1978 and joined the university full time in 1981. He served as a researcher and director of the Edwards Accelerator Laboratory, was named a Distinguished Professor in 2001 and became Distinguished Professor Emeritus in 2011. He is a Fellow of the American Physical Society.
