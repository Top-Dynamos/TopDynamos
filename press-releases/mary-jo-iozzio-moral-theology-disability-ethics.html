Mary Jo Iozzio, PhD, STL, Advances Moral Theology Through Disability Justice, Inclusion and Catholic Social Ethics

Boston College Professor of Moral Theology Mary Jo Iozzio has built a distinguished academic career examining disability ethics, Catholic social teaching, feminist ethics, intersectionality and the moral responsibilities of communities toward people historically pushed to the margins.

Boston CollegeBoston, MASeptember 22, 2026

Dynamo Times highlights Mary Jo Iozzio, PhD, STL, Professor of Moral Theology at Boston College's Clough School of Theology and Ministry, whose career has brought disability justice and inclusion into sustained conversation with Catholic moral theology.

Since joining Boston College in 2013, Dr. Iozzio has taught, researched and written on questions involving Catholic social ethics, disability ethics, feminist ethics, intersectionality and ethics from the margins. Her work asks a fundamental moral question: how should institutions and communities respond to people whose dignity and participation have too often been overlooked? For Dr. Iozzio, disability is not a peripheral issue in ethics. It belongs within the central conversation about human dignity, justice, community and flourishing. In March 2026, Marquis Who's Who announced her inclusion in Who's Who in the World, recognizing her longstanding academic and professional contributions.

Dr. Iozzio has spent decades teaching and writing in moral theology. Her academic work combines theological reflection with contemporary questions involving justice, disability, marginalization and social responsibility. Moral theology considers how people and communities should live, make decisions and respond to ethical responsibilities. Dr. Iozzio's scholarship brings those questions into direct conversation with people and communities whose experiences have historically received insufficient attention in theological discourse.

Dr. Iozzio's academic preparation spans history, religion, biblical studies and theology. She earned a Bachelor of Arts from Pennsylvania State University, where her studies included history with a concentration involving ancient Greece and Rome. She continued graduate study at Fordham University, earning a master's degree in the history of religions. She later earned a Master of Arts in Biblical Studies from Providence College. In 1994, she completed her PhD at Fordham University in Systematic Theology with a focus on Moral Theology. This multidisciplinary preparation created a foundation for a career examining moral questions through historical, biblical and theological perspectives.

In 2019, Dr. Iozzio earned the Licentiate in Sacred Theology (STL) from Boston College, with a focus on disability. The credential further developed an area that had already become central to her scholarly identity. Her work on disability challenges assumptions about whose experiences are treated as normative and whose voices are placed at the edges of academic, religious and social life. By bringing disability studies into moral theology, Dr. Iozzio has helped expand the questions theologians ask about justice, dependence, participation and human flourishing.

Before joining Boston College, Dr. Iozzio spent approximately two decades at Barry University in Miami Shores, Florida, serving as a professor from 1993 through 2013. Her years at Barry were devoted to teaching, scholarship and mentorship. The period established her as an experienced theological educator and provided the setting for continued development of her work in moral theology and social ethics. She also held adjunct teaching roles at institutions including Fordham University, Providence College and the University of Rhode Island.

Dr. Iozzio joined the School of Theology and Ministry at Boston College in 2013. Boston College currently identifies her as Professor of Moral Theology and Professora Ordinaria. Her listed research interests include Catholic social teaching and social ethics, feminist ethics, disability ethics and ethics from the margins. Her areas of expertise include moral theology and Christian ethics, global Catholicism, and feminist and liberation theologies. These areas converge around questions of dignity, justice and participation.

One of the defining themes of Dr. Iozzio's career is disability ethics. Her scholarship argues for taking the lives and experiences of people with disabilities seriously within theological and ethical reflection. This requires more than simply discussing disability as a medical condition. It means considering accessibility, social participation, relationships, institutional structures and the ways assumptions about ability can influence moral thinking. Dr. Iozzio's work has helped make disability a more visible subject within Catholic moral theology.

In 2023, Georgetown University Press published Dr. Iozzio's book "Disability Ethics and Preferential Justice: A Catholic Perspective." The book introduces disability ethics through a Catholic framework and argues for intentional attention to the justice concerns of people and communities with disabilities. It examines disability realities, inclusion and theological understandings of human dignity while offering practical approaches to creating more participatory communities. The work has subsequently been reviewed in academic journals including the Journal of Moral Theology and The Journal of Religion.

A recurring theological idea in Dr. Iozzio's scholarship is the imago Dei: the belief that human beings are created in the image of God. Within disability ethics, this concept has important implications. If dignity is inherent rather than dependent on physical ability, productivity or independence, then communities must examine structures and attitudes that exclude people with disabilities. Dr. Iozzio's work uses this theological framework to challenge narrow assumptions about human value.

Disability theology can also challenge cultural assumptions about independence. Human beings depend on other people throughout life: children depend on caregivers, adults depend on communities and social systems, and aging can increase the need for assistance. Dr. Iozzio's scholarship explores dependence not simply as a limitation but as part of the reality of human relationships. This perspective creates space for ethical discussions centered on solidarity rather than isolation.

Dr. Iozzio's work extends beyond disability studies. Boston College identifies feminist ethics among her principal research interests, while her teaching and scholarship also engage intersectionality. Intersectional analysis considers how different dimensions of identity and social experience can overlap. Disability may intersect with gender, economic status, race, age and other social conditions. Ethical reflection becomes more complete when those overlapping realities are considered rather than treating every person's experience as identical.

Catholic social teaching provides another major framework for Dr. Iozzio's scholarship. Themes such as human dignity, solidarity, participation, care for vulnerable communities and the common good connect closely with disability justice. Dr. Iozzio's work asks what these principles require in practice. If a community affirms human dignity, how accessible is that community? If it values participation, who is absent? If it promotes solidarity, whose experiences are not being heard? These questions move ethics from abstract principle toward institutional and social practice.

Boston College lists "Ethics from the Margins" among Dr. Iozzio's research interests. The phrase reflects an approach that pays particular attention to perspectives that dominant institutions can overlook. Rather than beginning only with the assumptions of those who hold social power, ethics from the margins asks what moral questions look like from the perspective of people who encounter exclusion. This orientation is closely connected with Dr. Iozzio's work on disability, feminist ethics and Catholic social thought.

In 2025, the Journal of Religious Ethics published Dr. Iozzio's review essay, "Mainstreaming Disability Theology." The article surveyed recent scholarship in disability studies and religious ethics while considering the increasing recognition of disability ethics within the field. The essay reflects a significant development in the discipline. After decades in which disability theology had to argue for its place in academic conversation, scholars are increasingly treating it as an established and necessary area of inquiry. Dr. Iozzio has been part of that development.

Dr. Iozzio's contribution to scholarship has also included editorial work. Her professional profile notes that she previously served for more than five years as co-editor of the Journal of the Society of Christian Ethics. In 2026, she began serving as series editor for "Thriving: A Series of Monographs and Collection of Essays," published by Bloomsbury. Editorial leadership provides another way for scholars to influence a discipline: editors help identify emerging questions, support other researchers and shape the conversations that enter academic publication.

Dr. Iozzio has maintained active involvement in organizations including the Catholic Theological Society of America, the American Academy of Religion and the Society of Christian Ethics. Professional associations create opportunities for scholars to exchange research, debate ideas and develop collaborative relationships. For an interdisciplinary field such as disability theology, these connections can help bring ethical questions into conversations across theology, religious studies and social justice.

Dr. Iozzio has presented her work at universities and professional conferences. Her professional profile identifies presentations at Fordham University, Georgetown University's Kennedy Institute of Ethics, the Institute on Theology and Disability at Boston College and meetings of the Society of Christian Ethics. These presentations extend scholarship beyond published pages: lectures and conferences allow researchers to test ideas, respond to questions and engage directly with other scholars and practitioners.

Dr. Iozzio's ethical interests also include environmental and climate justice. In 2021, she was selected for the inaugural Boston College delegation to the COP26 United Nations climate conference. Climate ethics intersects with broader questions about justice because environmental harm does not affect every community equally. People who already experience social or economic marginalization may face additional barriers when responding to climate-related disruption. Her involvement demonstrates the breadth of her engagement with contemporary moral questions.

Dr. Iozzio's commitment to justice extends beyond academic writing. Her professional profile describes involvement in church ministry, food-bank volunteering, environmental activities and social-justice advocacy. She has also participated in advisory and nonprofit work. These activities reflect an effort to connect theological principles with lived community engagement. For a scholar of moral theology, this connection between reflection and action is significant.

In 2018, Dr. Iozzio received the St. Elizabeth Ann Seton Medal from Mount St. Joseph University. The recognition became one of several honors associated with her academic and professional work. Her career has also included professional-development awards during her years at Barry University and later recognition for her influence within her field. These honors reflect sustained contributions across teaching, scholarship and service.

One theme appearing in Dr. Iozzio's professional profile is the importance of presuming goodwill and cultivating a welcoming spirit. This principle complements her academic emphasis on inclusion. A welcoming institution does more than state that everyone belongs: it examines whether its physical spaces, practices, language and expectations actually allow people to participate. Within disability ethics, the distinction between symbolic welcome and practical accessibility can be especially important.

Dr. Iozzio remains active in research and writing. Her recent scholarship continues to address disability theology and disability justice, including questions surrounding accessibility. Her future academic interests also include returning to fundamental moral theology and examining theological virtues through the work of Belgian scholar Odon Lottin. This continuing research demonstrates that a long academic career can remain intellectually dynamic.

In March 2026, Marquis Who's Who announced Dr. Iozzio's inclusion in Who's Who in the World. The announcement highlighted her academic career, disability scholarship, publications, professional organizations, community involvement and commitment to social justice. The recognition provides another public milestone in a career whose primary impact has developed through teaching, research, writing and advocacy.

Across Dr. Iozzio's work, a consistent question emerges: what allows people to flourish? The answer involves more than individual achievement. Flourishing also depends on whether communities create conditions where people can participate, belong and be treated with dignity. Disability ethics makes those conditions visible. It asks institutions to examine barriers rather than assuming the individual is the problem. It asks communities to value interdependence rather than treating complete independence as the measure of human worth. And it asks moral theology to listen carefully to people whose experiences have historically been pushed to the margins.

Dynamo Times recognizes Mary Jo Iozzio, PhD, STL, for her contributions to moral theology, disability ethics, Catholic social teaching, education and the continuing work of building more inclusive communities.

About Boston College

Mary Jo Iozzio, PhD, STL, is Professor of Moral Theology and Professora Ordinaria at Boston College's Clough School of Theology and Ministry. Her research interests include Catholic social teaching and social ethics, feminist ethics, disability ethics and ethics from the margins. She earned a BA from Pennsylvania State University, graduate degrees from Fordham University and Providence College, a PhD in Systematic Theology with a focus on Moral Theology from Fordham University and an STL from Boston College. Before joining Boston College in 2013, she served as a professor at Barry University from 1993 to 2013. She is the author of "Disability Ethics and Preferential Justice: A Catholic Perspective" (Georgetown University Press, 2023) and has published extensively on disability, moral theology, bioethics and social justice.
