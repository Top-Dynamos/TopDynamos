Jean Mineo Leads Bellforge Arts Center's Transformation of a Historic Medfield Campus Into a Regional Home for Arts and Community

Nonprofit arts leader Jean Mineo is guiding Bellforge Arts Center in Medfield, Massachusetts, through an ambitious cultural redevelopment combining historic preservation, performing arts, education, community engagement and expanded access to creative experiences.

Bellforge Arts Center / Cultural Alliance of MedfieldMedfield, MassachusettsAugust 28, 2026

MEDFIELD, Mass.: Dynamo Times highlights Jean Mineo, Executive Director of Bellforge Arts Center, whose career in nonprofit arts leadership has centered on expanding access to creative experiences and strengthening connections among artists, audiences and communities.

Mineo is helping lead the transformation of historic buildings on the former Medfield State Hospital campus into a year-round arts and education destination. In June 2026, Bellforge broke ground on a $30 million historic preservation and renovation project involving the former Lee Chapel and adjacent Infirmary. Plans call for a flexible 300-seat performance venue, 17 practice rooms and classrooms, and a new glass connector serving as a central lobby and gallery.

For Mineo, the mission extends beyond renovating buildings. Her work reflects a belief that meaningful cultural experiences should be accessible outside major metropolitan centers and that the arts can create lasting community connections.

Mineo holds a Bachelor of Fine Arts from Rochester Institute of Technology and a Master of Arts in Arts Administration from American University. Her career includes extensive experience managing contemporary galleries and nonprofit community arts organizations, along with cultural management and public-art project work.

Her professional development reinforced an important idea: the arts can have their greatest impact when people feel invited to participate. After experience with a national arts agency, her work in community-based arts strengthened her commitment to accessible, community-centered cultural experiences.

From 2006 through 2015, Mineo served as Director of the Boston Sculptors Gallery, working within Boston's contemporary arts community and directly with professional artists. She also ran Jamaica Plain Open Studios for three years, helping connect artists directly with the public. These experiences strengthened her understanding of how arts organizations can create meaningful bridges between working artists and surrounding communities.

Mineo has been a Medfield resident since 2004. She served as Chair of the Medfield Cultural Council, supporting local cultural programming and access to the arts. In 2015, the Massachusetts Cultural Council recognized her with a Leadership Circle Award. In 2017, she was honored as Medfield's Volunteer of the Year. Her local involvement helped establish a foundation for the cultural-development work that would follow.

In 2015, Mineo helped establish the Cultural Alliance of Medfield to nurture and support cultural programs, encourage collaboration and experimentation, and foster the arts as a cultural necessity. Bellforge Arts Center operates as a DBA of the Cultural Alliance of Medfield. The organization became the institutional foundation for a larger vision: transforming part of the former Medfield State Hospital campus into a regional cultural destination.

Bellforge's vision combines historic preservation with contemporary cultural use. Transforming the former hospital buildings requires far more than artistic programming. It involves fundraising, preservation, construction planning, municipal relationships, community engagement and long-term organizational strategy. As Executive Director, Mineo works at the intersection of these responsibilities, helping translate a cultural vision into a permanent physical institution.

Bellforge began offering outdoor programming before its permanent buildings were ready, presenting concerts, festivals, workshops and community experiences on the campus. According to Bellforge, outdoor programming since 2022 had attracted more than 42,000 visitors by the organization's June 2026 groundbreaking announcement. The programming allowed the public to experience the site while long-term redevelopment progressed and demonstrated demand for a regional arts destination.

In June 2026, Bellforge broke ground on a $30 million historic preservation and renovation project. The Lee Chapel is planned as a flexible 300-seat performance venue. The adjacent Infirmary is planned to contain 17 practice rooms and classrooms. A glass connector will serve as a central lobby and gallery. Bellforge has announced a target opening in fall 2027. The project represents a major transition from seasonal outdoor programming toward a permanent, year-round cultural institution.

Access is a recurring theme in Mineo's work. Cultural opportunities can be concentrated in major cities, creating practical barriers for people elsewhere. Bellforge seeks to expand access to professional performances, education, community programming and direct artistic participation within the MetroWest region. Mineo's leadership emphasizes creating an environment where broad audiences feel welcome to experience and participate in the arts.

The educational component of Bellforge is central to its long-term plans. Practice rooms and classrooms are designed to provide opportunities for learning, rehearsal, instruction and creative development in addition to public performances. A cultural center can serve audiences while also helping develop future artists and giving community members opportunities to participate directly in creative activity.

Bellforge's project demonstrates how cultural development can intersect with historic preservation. The Lee Chapel and Infirmary were originally part of the Medfield State Hospital campus. Adapting them for contemporary cultural use preserves historic architecture while giving the buildings a new public purpose. Bellforge received preservation-related support during the redevelopment process, including a 2024 matching grant supporting exterior restoration of the former Infirmary.

Large cultural projects require significant financial resources and sustained organizational leadership. Bellforge previously secured major support from organizations including the Barr Foundation, Mass Cultural Council and MassDevelopment. In 2022, the organization announced $1.475 million in grants from those organizations for programming, community engagement and development. Mineo's work demonstrates how nonprofit leadership requires balancing creative vision with fundraising, organizational development and practical implementation.

A project of Bellforge's scale depends on relationships among nonprofit leaders, artists, donors, municipal officials, preservation advocates, community members, architects, contractors and cultural partners. Mineo's career has repeatedly involved this type of collaboration. Her work on local cultural initiatives, public art and nonprofit organizations reflects an approach centered on bringing different groups into a shared project.

Mineo's leadership reflects a view of the arts as more than entertainment. Concerts, workshops, festivals, public art and educational programs can create shared experiences while providing artists with opportunities to present work, young people with opportunities to learn and communities with spaces for meaningful connection. Bellforge's development represents an effort to make those experiences a permanent part of the region's cultural infrastructure.

In 2026, Mineo was profiled by Influential Women for her leadership at Bellforge Arts Center and her work in nonprofit arts and community development. The profile highlighted her academic background, cultural leadership and commitment to diversity, inclusion, collaboration and community engagement.

With construction underway, Bellforge is entering a significant new phase. The permanent campus is intended to create year-round opportunities for performance, education and artistic participation. For Mineo, the project brings together themes that have shaped her career: arts administration, cultural access, preservation, collaboration and community leadership.

Dynamo Times recognizes Jean Mineo for her contributions to nonprofit arts leadership, cultural access, historic preservation and community development, and for helping guide Bellforge Arts Center toward its next chapter as a regional cultural destination.

About Bellforge Arts Center / Cultural Alliance of Medfield

Jean Mineo is Executive Director of Bellforge Arts Center in Medfield, Massachusetts. She has experience managing contemporary galleries and nonprofit community arts organizations, with expertise in cultural management and public-art project management. Mineo served as Director of Boston Sculptors Gallery from 2006 to 2015 and ran Jamaica Plain Open Studios for three years. She holds a BFA from Rochester Institute of Technology and an MA in Arts Administration from American University. A Medfield resident since 2004, she previously served as Chair of the Medfield Cultural Council, received a Massachusetts Cultural Council Leadership Circle Award in 2015 and was named Medfield's Volunteer of the Year in 2017. She helped establish the Cultural Alliance of Medfield in 2015 and today leads Bellforge's continuing development as an arts, education and community destination.
